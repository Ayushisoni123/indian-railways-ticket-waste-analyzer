Case 1 & 2: Full-Journey Priority + Exact Short-Leg Auto-Swap

Full-journey buyers (MAS → NDLS) get early priority. Closer to departure (T-6h to T-1h), a partial-leg buyer (MAS → NGP) receives immediate travel confirmation while remaining in a berth-assignment buffer; if an exact MAS → NGP ticket is flagged later, CRIS auto-swaps them (respecting Lower Berth locks) and frees the long-haul seat intact.

Case 3 & 4: Complementary Leg Pairing & Post-Chart Settlement

If a complementary NGP → NDLS buyer arrives, both legs share one physical berth across two separate station-indexed PNRs. If the second leg stays unsold at T-30m, the flagger receives the MAS → NGP portion at Second Chart Freeze — guaranteeing ₹0 cash loss to Indian Railways.

Why True Station-Indexed PNR Splitting & v1/v2 Scoping Are Mandatory
  • Prevents False No-Show (NTU) Cancellation: In CRIS/HHT, leaving a second-leg buyer (NGP → NDLS) on an unsplit MAS → NDLS PNR would cause the Chennai TTE to mark the berth empty at origin and reallocate it to RAC before Nagpur. Splitting into two station-indexed PNRs displays Ramesh (MAS→NGP) then Mahesh (NGP→NDLS) on the TTE HHT.
  • Why Ship v1 End-to-End First: Validating single-leg transfer adoption, Aadhaar-OTP rate limits, and T-1h HHT sync on the Southern Railway (SR) Express pilot first avoids mid-journey station-halt handover friction during MVP validation.
